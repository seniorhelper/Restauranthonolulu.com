FAQ

Straight answers about restaurants in Honolulu

FAQ for dining in Honolulu and Waikiki — best neighborhoods, reservations, poke, plate lunch, costs, and how this guide works.

What are the best restaurants in Honolulu right now?

It depends what you want the night to be. For Hawaiian food with history, Helena's Hawaiian Food in Kalihi is the reference point. For a Waikiki sunset, House Without a Key still does the thing people come to Hawaii hoping to feel. For a chef's counter, Chinatown's Senia is the reservation that takes planning. This guide keeps the public list short on purpose.

What are the best restaurants in Waikiki?

Waikiki restaurants split into three useful buckets: oceanfront for the view, hotel dining that's actually good, and the walkable casual rooms you use for breakfast and poke. If you only have one special dinner on the strip, book sunset and eat your ambitious meal somewhere quieter the next night.

Do I need a reservation at Honolulu restaurants?

For tasting menus, Saturday fine dining, and the famous sunset rooms — yes, often a week or more out. For plate lunch, poke counters, food trucks, and most neighborhood lunch spots, no. Waikiki walk-ins are real at 5 p.m. and a fantasy at 7:15 p.m.

Is Honolulu expensive to eat in?

A plate lunch or food-truck meal still lands roughly $12–20. Casual sit-down runs $25–50 a person before drinks. Oceanfront dinner and tasting menus are special-occasion money. Honolulu is generous if you eat like a local at lunch and spend up once.

What Hawaiian food should I try in Honolulu?

Plate lunch (two scoops rice, mac salad, a protein), poke, kalua pig, lomi salmon, and poi if you want the real grammar of the table. Helena's is the classic classroom.

Where do locals eat in Honolulu?

Kaimuki, Kapahulu, Kalihi, Chinatown, and Kakaako more than the Waikiki strip. Locals still eat in Waikiki — they just don't treat it as the whole city.
