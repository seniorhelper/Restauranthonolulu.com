    Three days of eating in Honolulu

    Each day stays in one part of town, so you’re not driving all day. Check hours on each page — several places close one or two days a week.

    Day 1 · Waikīkī & Kapahulu

    Breakfast
    Liliha Bakery Waikīkī Beach Walk (coco puffs, griddle breakfasts) or musubi to go from Musubi Cafe Iyasume.
    Lunch
    Poke from Ono Seafood, then malasadas at Leonard's Bakery two blocks away.
    Afternoon
    Shave ice at Waiola Shave Ice Kapahulu.
    Sunset
    A drink at House Without a Key (book ahead) or Duke's Waikiki.
    Dinner
    Hy's Steak House for a classic steak room, or udon at Marugame Udon Waikīkī if you want cheap and fast.

    Day 2 · Chinatown, Downtown & Kalihi

    Breakfast
    Dim sum at Legend Seafood Restaurant — go before 10 am on weekends.
    Lunch
    Hawaiian food at Helena's Hawaiian Food (closed weekends and Mondays — check hours), or plate lunch at Alicia's Market.
    Snack
    Manjū and mochi at Nisshodo Candy Store.
    Dinner
    Fête or the tasting menu at Senia (reserve).
    Late
    Cocktails at Bar Leather Apron.

    Day 3 · Kaimukī & Kāhala

    Brunch
    Koko Head Cafe or Café Kaila — expect a wait after 9 am.
    Lunch
    Tonkatsu at Tonkatsu Tamafuji.
    Dinner
    Mud Hen Water, The Pig & the Lady, or Alan Wong's at The Kāhala for a big night.

    Day trip · North Shore

    On the way
    Breakfast at Kono's in Haleʻiwa.
    Lunch
    Garlic shrimp at Giovanni's Shrimp Truck in Kahuku.
    Afternoon
    Matsumoto Shave Ice, then the farm café at Kahuku Farms Café.
